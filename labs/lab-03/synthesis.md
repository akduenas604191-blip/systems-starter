## 2. Evidence ladder classification

**Said:** the person describes or states it. **Did:** the person took action. **Paid:** the person spent money (or committed budget).

| # | Problem | Rung | Why |
|---|---------|------|-----|
| P1 | Missed deadlines lead to auto-approval and skip compliance | **Said** | Describes the problem. Mentions no action of their own. |
| P2 | Hard to get employees to submit | **Said** | Names the difficulty. |
| P3 | Happens often | **Said** | One-word frequency claim with no number given. |
| P4 | Email reminders don't work | **Did** | They tried email outreach: "Reaching out via email…" |
| P5 | Manual follow-up takes time | **Did** | They actively do this: "i have to individually reach out." The time cost is described as "a lot," not measured. |
| P6 | Employees lose reimbursement money | **Said** | Reported about other people (secondhand). |
| P7 | Reminder help shouldn't be needed | **Said** | A wish ("wave a wand"). |

**Paid:** Nothing in the notes shows money spent or budget committed.

---

## 3. Job-to-be-done

> **When I'm** waiting on employees to submit expense reports before the deadline, **I want to** get them to submit without extra reminder help, **so I can** keep reports from being auto-approved around the compliance measures put in place.

---

## 4. Current habit and its cost

**What they do today:**
- The system flags the late report and sends its own email.
- If that email gets no response, the interviewee follows up with each employee individually.
- They have reached out via email.

**What it costs:**
- **Time:** "It costs me a lot of time" (not measured).
- **Effectiveness:** email outreach "was not effective in pushing expense report submission."
- **Compliance:** missed deadlines still "trigger auto approval, completely avoiding all compliance measures put in place."
- **Employees:** they are "losing out on the money that they spend and need to reimbursed for."
- **Frequency:** it happens "often."

---

## 5. Three questions I should have asked

1. **"How much time did the follow-up take last month, in hours, and for how many employees?"** "A lot of time" and "often" have no numbers, so the pain can't be sized.
2. **"Walk me through the last time this happened. Who missed it, how did you reach them, and what happened next?"** The notes are general. One specific recent case would show the real steps, including which channel the individual outreach used, which the notes don't say.
3. **"Have you spent money or asked for budget to fix this, such as a tool, a policy change, or extra staff?"** This is the only way to test the **paid** rung, and right now there is no evidence for it.
